Mother tongue (L1) interference and HVPT theory

Adult L2 learners perceive foreign speech through the phonological grid of their native language (L1). Modern Greek has a symmetrical 5-vowel system (/i, e, a, o, u/) and 15 consonants, whereas Southern British English (RP) has 20 vowels and 24 consonants.

1. Single-category merger: why Greek merges "sea" and "she" (PAM-L2)

Standard Modern Greek has no postalveolar /ʃ/. Because Greek /s/ has no postalveolar competitor, it is articulated as a retracted [s̠] with a spectral peak around 4.2–5.5 kHz, sitting right in the acoustic middle between sharp English /s/ (6–8 kHz) and lip-rounded /ʃ/ (2.5–3.8 kHz). Both English sounds fall inside one Greek perceptual magnet.

2. Vowel space starvation and the "empty center"

Greek has no mid-central vowels (/ɜː, ə/) and no /ʌ/ (its only central vowel is open /a/), and lacks tense/lax pairs (/ɪ–iː/, /ʊ–uː/) and open-front /æ/. English /ɜː/ ("bird") scatters toward Greek /e, o, a/, while /æ, ʌ, ɑː/ crowd into Greek /a/.

3. How this studio breaks L1 magnets (SLM-r and HVPT)

  • Cross-token testing: Uses alternate 1994 recordings (*B.WAV, *C.WAV) so you learn abstract phoneme categories rather than memorizing one audio clip.
  • Immediate contrastive replay: Mistakes trigger side-by-side playback within echoic memory.
  • Spectral biofeedback: Real-time FFT and spectral centroid metering let your eyes inspect acoustic cues (such as 3 kHz vs. 7 kHz sibilants) while your ear recalibrates.

4. Transcription convention

Transcriptions follow the standard British dictionary convention (J. C. Wells, Longman Pronunciation Dictionary; Jones & Roach, Cambridge English Pronouncing Dictionary): /e/ = DRESS (bed), /eə/ = SQUARE (chair).

How to use this studio

In the original 1994 ToolBook 3 guide: "Click on any WORD button to listen to it or on a PLUS (+) button to hear a pair of words. Try to repeat what you are hearing."

  • Explore & contrast: Listen to individual words, click + for immediate minimal-pair playback, and inspect Token B / Token C alternate recordings where available.
  • Identification test: Hear a target word and click the matching word (shortcuts: 1, 2, 3, and R to replay).
  • AX same / different: Judge whether two tokens belong to the same phonemic word or contrasting words (shortcuts: 1/S for Same, 2/D for Different).
  • Record & compare: Record your own pronunciation and compare it on the spectrum meter and via sequential Native → You → Native playback.
